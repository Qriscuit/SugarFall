# Sugarfall — first player experience

Design revision: 26 October 2026. Working character name: **Mabel**.

This document records the first playable introduction: meet the host, aim a real tray, earn coins, collect a time circle when it first appears, collect the result, buy the first feeder upgrade, and start another tray. The implementation and verification record at the end distinguishes delivered behavior from later ideas.

## What the player should understand

Within one short play-and-upgrade loop, the player should be able to answer four questions without opening Help:

1. **What am I looking at?** A bakery tray, falling donuts, breakable cookies, and collection cups.
2. **What do I do?** Drag across the tray to aim the automatic feeder; break every cookie before the timer runs out. Hover over a time circle, or touch or drag onto it, for extra time.
3. **Why do I keep playing?** Hits and catches earn coins, even on an unfinished tray. Collect the earnings and improve the equipment.
4. **What do I do next?** Start another tray with the upgrade and earn toward the next improvement.

The narrative is one sentence: **“Let's build a little bakery, one tray at a time.”** Mabel is the welcoming baker showing the player how her unusual bakery works. This supplies a reason to improve without requiring a cutscene, backstory, customers, dialogue choices, or a second simulation system.

The opening teaches the actual game. The feeder already drops automatically; there is no invented tap-to-shoot control. The first purchase is Feed 1, currently 38 coins under the shipped tuning. Copy and gating must use `upgrade_cost(0)` so future tuning does not make the tutorial lie. More drops create more opportunities to hit cookies and catch food; they do not guarantee a specific payout or a clear.

## Reference findings and our design decisions

Flo is the host character associated with **Diner Dash**. The official *Diner Dash: Sizzle and Serve* manual describes a guided first restaurant level in which customers remain happy, then explains new objects as they appear. It also frames play around helping Flo operate a small restaurant. These are documented patterns, not a claim that every Diner Dash edition has an identical tutorial. [Official manual, printed pages 2 and 8](https://support.na.square-enix.com/document/manual/1022/dd_ds.pdf).

**Cooking Academy** frames its activities as learning cooking skills through courses and recipes. The publisher describes preparing recipes and progressing through courses and exams. That supports a recognizable mentor-and-lesson tone; this source does not establish the precise opening UI or character animation. [Big Fish's Cooking Academy page](https://www.bigfishgames.com/cooking-academy-f2398t1l1.html).

Our design inference is to provide a friendly host, a small reason to care, and one meaningful action at a time. Explanation holds the game clock; practice uses the real game. A circular focus directs attention to the object being discussed. Sugarfall keeps its existing bakery setting and develops an original host, rather than naming her Flo or importing another game's character art.

## Mabel and the dialogue presentation

Mabel should feel like a capable, friendly bakery owner. Her expression is warm and attentive; her hand presents the board or opens toward the next action. The initial asset is an isolated, transparent portrait with enough torso and gesture to read at phone size. Match Sugarfall's softly painted 2.5D forms, upper-left warm lighting, mint, cream and copper material family, and clean silhouette. Follow [ART_DIRECTION.md](ART_DIRECTION.md).

The portrait and speech bubble are separate layers. The image contains no words, labels, prices, controls, or opaque background. Godot draws Mabel's name, the dialogue and the active action. This allows text changes and preserves crisp type at different screen sizes. The portrait is loaded from `data/art/host/mabel_welcome.png`.

The current speech bubble floats beside Mabel and fits its message. [speech_bubble.gd](../scripts/speech_bubble.gd) measures the complete dialogue, name and the wider of the reveal and ready interaction hints before revealing characters. Its bounds stay fixed throughout that message, including when “Tap anywhere to reveal” becomes “Tap anywhere to continue,” so the typewriter does not resize or reflow the balloon. The guide caches those metrics per message, step and layout options. The cream balloon has rounded corners, a small shadow and a curved tail that reaches beside Mabel's mouth; there is no divider line or oversized empty panel.

The reusable layout currently uses a 264–410 logical-pixel width, 23-pixel padding, 26-pixel body type and measured line spacing of at least 33 pixels. Height comes from the actual wrapped lines plus the compact name and hint. Long tokens can wrap too. Mabel occupies an independent, aspect-preserving 220 × 330 slot; short dialogue never shrinks the character. Each new bubble scales outward from its mouth anchor over 0.22 seconds. Reduced Motion draws the final size and complete text immediately.

Placement follows the action: Home dialogue stays above Play, the shop dialogue stays above Feed 1, the collection dialogue stays above Collect, and the aiming lesson sits below the draggable tray region. Bubble, portrait and tail must all preserve that clearance. The close-up and spotlight remain separate layers and keep pointing at the real gameplay object or control.

- Keep each message to one idea, usually two short sentences.
- Reveal text at 60 characters per second. Tap anywhere inside the game canvas to complete an appearing line. On the four dialogue-only beats (`welcome`, `aim`, `earned`, `improved`), a later deliberate tap anywhere advances the instruction. Reduced Motion reveals the line immediately.
- Skip and Pause keep their own actions and take priority over dialogue. A tap must finish as one contact with little movement, inside the same target region; canceled touches, drags, multiple fingers and taps during the navigation guard do not advance a line. Mouse clicks use the existing emulated-touch path, avoiding duplicate advancement.
- Keep the whole bubble visible long enough to read. Never auto-advance a learning instruction after a timer.
- During action steps, a background tap only completes the appearing text; it cannot finish the lesson. The highlighted control or gesture performs the actual game action. A generic “Next” must not claim the player has aimed, collected, selected, or purchased. A touch can still start on the background and drag onto the time circle, while a gesture that begins on the speech bubble remains a dialogue interaction.
- Mabel moves out of the way while the player plays. The guide should not obscure falling food, the aim area, the timer or the result collection action.
- Preserve a visible Skip action. Skip dismisses guidance and restores normal control; it never spends, grants, collects or resets anything.

## First-session play-by-play

The timings below are design targets for a person who reads at a normal pace, not measured usability results. The sequence is event-driven. A slow reader must not lose play time, and a fast reader must not wait for a staged animation to finish before continuing.

| Beat | What appears and where attention goes | Mabel's runtime message | Player action and exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Meet Mabel (`welcome`) | The real Home tray remains visible. Mabel enters with a short welcome. | “Hi, I'm Mabel! Let's build a little bakery, one tray at a time.” | Tap anywhere to reveal the line, then tap anywhere again to continue to the Play instruction. No run clock exists yet. |
| 2. Bring up the board (`play`) | Circular focus marks the existing Play button. The player still sees the tray it will open. | “Our first tray is ready. Tap Play and I'll show you the recipe.” | Tap the real Play action. Use the existing Home-to-board expansion. The transition remains frozen gameplay. |
| 3a. Learn the one gesture (`aim`) | The expanded board is held still. Focus and a circular magnifier show the dispenser. | “Donuts drop on their own. You steer the dispenser by dragging across the tray.” | Tap anywhere to reveal or continue to the gesture prompt. Mabel stays clear of the drag area; Pause and Skip remain separate actions. |
| 3b. Try aiming (`aim_drag`) | The focus moves into the tray's aim region. | “Give it a try! Drag left or right across the tray to start baking.” | A deliberate horizontal drag in the highlighted board region moves the feeder and begins the attempt. Text taps do not count as aiming. |
| 4. See why hits matter (`earned`) | Let real food fall until the earned total reaches at least one whole coin. Briefly hold the run and emphasize its live earned total in a circular magnifier. | “That's money in the making! Cookie hits earn coins. Clear the tray before time runs out. You keep your earnings either way.” | Tap anywhere to reveal or continue. The trigger can be a hit or a cup catch. No coins are inserted for demonstration. |
| 5a. Collect the first time circle (`time_pickup`) | The first live time pickup during the guide holds the tray. A circular spotlight follows the actual collectible, which becomes 25% larger. Mabel stays below the tray. | “A time circle! Hover over it for up to 2 extra seconds. On a touchscreen, touch or drag over it. I've paused the tray for you.” | Move the mouse over the actual circle without clicking, or touch or drag onto it. Successful real collection adds the allowed time and resumes play. Dialogue taps and Enter/Space cannot complete this action. |
| 5b. Play a real tray | Mabel leaves the active play space. Each of the last three displayed seconds starts a single timer pop and alternating twist, then settles while its red color softens. | No further blocking dialogue after the pickup lesson. | Play through the actual attempt. A clear and a timeout both lead forward. A timeout is expected learning, not tutorial failure. |
| 6. Keep the earnings (`collect`) | On the actual result, show credits to collect, the previous-run earnings comparison, and two small stats; focus the Collect action while Mabel explains it. | “Every little batch helps. Collect your coins, then put them back into the bakery.” | Tap Collect once. Use the real payout, wallet precision, milestone and idempotency rules. The comparison describes the attempt and never changes its reward. |
| 7. Spend toward growth (`upgrades`) | Return to Home and focus Upgrades when Feed 1 is affordable. | “You've earned your first upgrade! Open Upgrades to improve the dispenser.” | Tap Upgrades. If the wallet is short, use the retry branch below instead. |
| 8. Choose Feed 1 (`feed`) | Frame the only initial recipe and highlight the Feed 1 tile. Keep the wallet and purchase card readable. | “This is Feed 1. Tap the dispenser recipe to see what it does.” | Select the actual Feed 1 tile. Selecting does not spend coins. |
| 9. Buy a real improvement (`buy`) | Focus the purchase action and leave its live price visible. | “More donuts, more chances to earn. Buy Feed 1 for {cost} coins.” | A successful real Feed 1 purchase advances the guide. A failed purchase does not. The three newly available routes appear through the normal map behavior. |
| 10a. Name the loop (`improved`) | The upgraded feeder is owned; the normal map reveals the next three recipes. | “Now your dispenser drops faster! Earn coins, improve your bakery, and take on the next tray.” | Tap anywhere to reveal or continue. No additional upgrade purchase is required. |
| 10b. Repeat (`again`) | Focus the map's Play button, or Home Play after a restart. | “Your bakery is in your hands. Tap Play and try your faster dispenser!” | Start the next real tray. Mark the first-run guide complete and release normal play. |

Aim for the first player interaction within roughly 5–10 seconds and a completed loop in roughly 45–75 seconds when the first attempt funds Feed 1. These targets need human testing. Do not force a duration by shrinking text, suppressing results, adding starter coins, reducing prices, or claiming a guaranteed clear.

### The time-circle moment and countdown

The pickup lesson is contextual, not a staged extra reward. A normal time circle appears after roughly four seconds of active play if the tray has not cleared and bonus time remains available. Its first appearance during the guide starts `time_pickup` and holds the simulation before the circle loses any of its four-second lifetime. The earned-coins explanation and the pickup explanation never replace each other while held. If both become eligible together, teach the live pickup first, then return to the earned-coins check. A tray that ends before any pickup appears can still progress normally; do not manufacture a collectible to complete a checklist.

The highlighted circle is the real collectible at its real board coordinates, transformed to the displayed board. Its art enlarges to 1.25 times the normal size during the lesson; there is one visible collectible target and no separate pickup magnifier. The existing collection radius remains 45 board pixels. Mouse hover requires no button; a direct touch or a touch-drag across the target also collects. A successful action is accepted immediately, including while the fast text reveal is finishing. A bubble tap can reveal the remaining text but cannot collect the circle or resume play. Skip remains available.

Collection uses the existing `collect_pickups()` and `add_time(2.0)` path, with the shared three-second cap for pickups and clock cookies. The words **“up to 2 extra seconds”** remain accurate when a replayed tray has already earned some clock-cookie time. The pickup is consumed once, its allowed time is applied once, and only then is `time_pickup_seen` saved with the updated run. Later circles in the same introduction do not repeat the explanation. Help replay and Reset progress clear the lesson flag.

The countdown becomes urgent only while `0 < remaining <= 3` and the tray is still active. It switches from the normal `00:03` style to **3s, 2s, 1s**, with red type inside a pale red badge. During uninterrupted countdown, each displayed decrement starts one size pop and twist, followed by a single settle. Normal type is 31 logical pixels; the urgent number starts at 84 and settles to 56 over 0.48 seconds. The twist alternates through −6°, +10° and −14° for 3s, 2s and 1s, then returns to upright. Its saturated red, icon and badge outline fade toward softer red over 0.95 seconds.

Each beat starts only when the displayed second decreases and then settles using active run time. Pause and held lessons freeze it automatically. A fractional bonus that keeps the same displayed number cannot rewind or regrow the beat. If a bonus increases the displayed number, that number remains settled until its next decrease. The helper keeps this presentation state only in memory and resets it for a new run. After restart, it reconstructs the current second's partial phase instead of replaying a full pop. Reduced Motion keeps a steady 56-pixel soft-red timer with no size, angle or color animation. Added time above three seconds restores the normal timer immediately; the urgency treatment is off during the final cleared-tray catch animation.

### The insufficient-coins branch

An arbitrary first aim can earn less than the cost of Feed 1. The prior [pacing study](PACING_v0.5.4.md) found its tested aiming policies could afford the first Feed after one attempt; it does not establish that every player will.

After Collect, compare the precise wallet with the live Feed 1 cost. If it is short, Mabel says: **“We need {shortfall} more coins for Feed 1. Try another tray — your earnings keep adding up!”** Use `credits_needed_text()` so the visible shortfall rounds upward from the precise balance. Highlight Play and allow another real attempt. Collect again, then re-evaluate affordability. Do not repeat the full welcome or the first-coin explanation. A concise retry instruction is enough. The player can Skip at any time.

If Feed 1 is already owned when the guide resumes, move to the upgraded replay beat instead of asking for a second purchase. In an explicit Help replay, the improvement message begins **“Your dispenser is already upgraded!”** rather than claiming a new upgrade was granted. If progression is incompatible with this first-Bakery lesson, release the guide safely rather than editing progression to fit the lesson.

### The end-of-run comparison

The campaign result answers three questions: what can I collect, how did these earnings compare with my previous attempt, and how much of the tray did I clear? Present it as a reward moment on an illustrated Bakery or Gelato counter. Its heading is **“Batch finished”**, **“Tray cleared!”** or **“Area complete!”**, with a small area/tray or unlock line. A ceramic dish of coins provides the visual focus; the dominant payout is live type placed directly over the scene. It shows the real visible wallet increase, including any bonus, wallet-fraction carry and applicable milestone conversion. The primary button simply says **“Collect”** and awards that amount under the existing payout rules. Do not repeat a “to collect” caption or the amount on the button. Avoid the previous stack of framed earnings, comparison and statistic panels.

Near the payout, a freestanding signed percentage compares **base run earnings with the previous finished run in the same area**. Its small qualifier is **“Run earnings vs last”**, or **“Run earnings”** without a previous result. Compute it from cent-precise `shift_coins`, excluding test-ad rewards, toolkit bonuses, milestone conversion and wallet rounding. A conditional **“Base {current} · last {previous}”** line appears when bonuses, wallet carry or fractional earnings need clarification; a first result uses **“Base {current}.”** Show up to one decimal and remove a redundant `.0`. A positive sign and green ink indicate more; a negative sign and red ink indicate less, so color is not the only direction cue. Tiny nonzero changes use **“<0.1% more”** or **“<0.1% less.”** Omit redundant headings and the base detail when it adds no information.

The first finished run has no comparison and shows neutral **“First run.”** Equal earnings, including zero to zero, show neutral **“0%.”** A positive result after zero shows green **“Up from 0”** without dividing by zero; positive earnings falling to zero correctly show **“−100%.”** When a current/previous base amount is displayed, positive earnings below one coin remain **“<1”**, preserving the distinction from true zero. The ordinary whole-coin wallet and payout calculations remain unchanged. Compare across trays and retries within an area, not only attempts on the same board. Bakery and Gelato Garden each have their own history.

Only two extra stats remain: **Cleared** as `destroyed / total`, with the area's cookie or wafer icon, and **Best chain** as `combo×`, with a chain icon. Each has a strong current value and a quiet **“Last …”** value when history exists. Place them directly on the calm illustrated surface, without cards or borders. Do not restore the redundant Tray statistic or four food-hit counters. Keep every result element clear of the main Collect action at y800 and the quieter **“Test bonus +N”** action at y910, which becomes **“Bonus added”** after a successful simulated reward. Their hitboxes remain unchanged, including the guide's Collect target. During Mabel's Collect instruction, her presentation can cover supplementary statistics while leaving Collect clear.

The result illustration and collectible dish are separate assets: `data/art/results/bakery_backdrop_v1.png`, `gelato_backdrop_v1.png` and `coin_dish_v1.png`. The area backdrops supply quiet central counter space and peripheral environmental detail. The shared dish retains transparent margins and natural proportions. No generated artwork contains labels, amounts, percentages or button copy; Godot supplies those live. See [the result art specification](ART_DIRECTION.md#results) and [the prompt record](../concept-art/results-v1/prompts.json). This composition changes presentation, not the comparison history or reward calculations described below.

`scripts/run_history.gd` keeps only the latest and previous finished campaign records for each area. Each record stores `run_id`, base `coins`, `destroyed`, `peg_total`, `combo` and `wave`. A campaign result records once when the run finishes, before the pending result is saved. Clears, timeouts and early Finish & Collect count; Trials never enter this history. Repeated saves, collection and loading the same pending result do not advance the history again because the latest run ID is already recorded. The two records retain the correct previous baseline when a finished result is reopened.

History lives in the optional root `run_history` object, version 1, separate from the area progression and interrupted-run schemas. Missing or invalid optional history is ignored without invalidating a valid campaign or inferring past earnings from the wallet. Old pending results can establish a baseline when loaded. Ordinary saves retain the history across restarts and area switches. Reset progress clears both areas' history only after the clean candidate save commits successfully.

## Circular focus and close-ups

The visual purpose is to answer “look here” without losing the screen's context. A translucent dark veil quiets unrelated content; a circular opening and a restrained mint/copper rim isolate the current object. The guide owns its input layer while an instruction is modal, so a visible opening does not accidentally activate a different control beneath it.

For the dispenser during `aim` and the actual earned coin total during `earned`, the implementation enlarges a live rendering inside a circular lens. A connector points back to the relevant screen region. The original board location remains understandable. This is a local magnifier, not a full-screen camera zoom. It does not capture a screenshot, substitute a sample reward, or change physics coordinates. The time-pickup lesson instead enlarges the actual collectible in place by 25%, inside a spotlight with a 76-pixel radius, preserving one clear action target and its normal collection radius.

For a button, focus its real bounds and keep the entire actionable label visible. For the upgrade tile, derive its location from `upgrade_map.node_rect(0, 1)` or `point(0, 1)` at the current map transform. Camera motion must not separate the highlight from the target. No decorative circle may cover the active purchase control or imply that an unaffordable recipe is available.

The bubble can move between upper and lower positions depending on the target. Its text, portrait and action must fit inside the logical safe area at both 720 × 1280 and the common 450 × 800 window. Keep character proportions intact; shorten copy or reflow it instead of shrinking functional text into illegibility.

Reduced Motion replaces animated entrances, spotlight rims and camera interpolation with their stable frames. The circle, readable text and progress cues remain. Dialogue should be usable with sound off. No voice performance is required for this iteration.

## State, interruptions and save behavior

The tutorial is presentation and guidance state around real campaign actions. It is not a separate economy, board ruleset, practice reward source, or reset mechanism.

- Store tutorial progress in the single campaign save. The root `onboarding` object carries `version`, `completed`, `active`, `step`, `earned_seen`, `time_pickup_seen`, and `upgraded_this_intro`. Existing version-one guide saves default the new pickup flag to false without invalidating their other progress. Main/Fresh Test selection and the queued Fresh Test welcome are retired.
- Automatically begin for a new Bakery campaign or after a confirmed successful Reset progress. Treat an existing pre-guide save as already introduced, even if it has little progression. Do not interrupt a returning player because an older save lacks the new field; Help offers an explicit replay.
- Persist a versioned, validated tutorial milestone separately from the strict interrupted-run snapshot. Missing or malformed optional tutorial data must not invalidate valid campaign progress.
- During held explanations, freeze physics, remaining time, pickup lifetime, automatic spawning, RNG advancement, active-play ad timing and checkpoint time. Presentation text and focus may continue animating.
- On focus loss or Back during play, keep the existing pause behavior. Resuming must not consume time while the explanatory overlay is still present.
- Reopen at a sensible pending beat based on both saved tutorial progress and actual campaign state. A saved result must still be collected once; a collected result must not be paid again; an owned Feed must not be bought twice.
- An interrupted pickup lesson restores its live target from the existing run snapshot and remains held after Resume. If the target is already gone, the lesson releases to play instead of waiting on an impossible action. A collected pickup remains consumed across reload; its saved completion flag suppresses repeat instruction. The field is optional guide metadata, not a new required run-snapshot field.
- Completing or skipping stops automatic guidance on ordinary reopening. Bakery's Help screen includes **Learn with Mabel** to replay the guide without resetting coins, upgrades, boards or test purchases. Replay uses the selected current tray. If Feed is already owned, collection leads to the improved/replay message without an additional purchase. Reset progress clears the campaign and starts the introduction as a new player.
- If a save is blocked or unavailable, preserve the game's existing error handling. Do not claim durable completion from a failed write.

Do not teach optional ads, test purchases, Trials, Treats, route strategy, gold multipliers, Gelato entry or every HUD symbol in the first minute. They can be introduced when the player actually reaches them. The time-circle pickup is the one contextual bonus taught during the opening because it appears during ordinary play and depends on a separate hover/touch action.

### Reset progress

**Settings → Reset progress** opens an explicit confirmation before any mutation. The confirmation explains that coins, upgrades, completed trays, all unlocked areas and trial records will be cleared, and that the player will restart at Bakery tray 1 with Mabel. It identifies Sound and Reduced Motion as the settings that remain. Cancel or Back returns without changing the save or campaign.

The confirmation uses **Cancel** and **Reset progress** as separate actions and says “This cannot be undone.” The Settings tap that opens it cannot also confirm: reset requires a new, completed single-contact tap. Dragging, canceled touches, multiple fingers and keyboard repeats do not confirm. Reset is entered from idle Settings; an active run, uncollected result or trial cannot be bypassed through it.

On confirmation, build and validate a fresh candidate save, then write it atomically. Apply it to the live game only after the write succeeds. A failed write leaves the current campaign intact and reports the failure. A successful reset removes area progress, wallets, upgrade tiers, equipped food, vouchers, milestone claims, trial records, pending runs/results, previous-run comparison history and tutorial completion. It also clears the explicitly simulated ad/purchase benefits, fake store ownership and ad-test state: there are no real paid purchases in this build. Internal request identities remain monotonic so an old callback cannot grant a post-reset benefit. The new Home opens on Bakery tray 1 with Mabel's welcome active.

This replaces the Main/Fresh Test profile feature. The player has one campaign and one canonical save, `user://sugarfall_save.json`, using the existing version 3 area schema. On the first updated launch, migration chooses the previously selected older campaign and preserves its progress rather than resetting it. Once written, the canonical save is authoritative. Old save files remain migration/recovery sources, not campaigns selectable from Settings. Updating the game does not itself authorize or execute a reset.

**Learn with Mabel** remains the non-destructive tutorial replay in Bakery. Reset progress is for starting the game over, not merely re-reading instructions.

## Acceptance checks

1. A new campaign sees Mabel automatically. An established save opens its existing Home, paused run or pending result without an unsolicited first-run reset.
2. The first explanation accurately says the feeder is automatic and the gesture is drag-to-aim.
3. While an explanatory beat holds a run, repeated frames do not advance timer, spawn clock, RNG, earned coins, ad time or active checkpoint time.
4. A tap anywhere in the logical canvas reveals an appearing line without also advancing it or activating a control behind the guide. A later tap anywhere advances only a dialogue-only beat. Action steps still require their actual action. Skip and Pause retain priority; off-canvas, dragged, canceled, multi-contact and navigation-guarded taps do not advance, and duplicate/emulated pointer input does not advance twice.
5. The first-coin moment uses actual earned coins. Both a cookie hit and a first cup catch can reach it without false copy.
6. Clear, timeout, early finish, focus loss and restart cannot leave the tutorial on an impossible screen or discard pending earnings.
7. Collect updates the real wallet once. Feed 1 selection spends nothing; its real successful purchase spends the actual current cost once.
8. A wallet below the live cost offers another tray. The guide neither grants coins nor soft-locks on an unaffordable button.
9. The Feed focus tracks the tile and the current map transform. Purchase feedback and branch reveal remain visible; the bubble does not hide the action it asks for.
10. Skip works from every held beat and mid-run. It releases guidance while preserving the current run, wallet and upgrades.
11. Completion and skipping survive restart in the single campaign save.
12. The portrait has real transparency, text remains live, and the guide is visually checked at both target sizes with normal and Reduced Motion presentation.
13. Bubble size follows the full message and stays unchanged during reveal and hint changes. Its name, text and hint fit without colliding; portrait size stays independent of text length. The tail originates beside Mabel's mouth and clears the real action.
14. Opening or canceling Reset progress does not change saved or live progress. Confirming commits a clean save before applying it, then starts Bakery tray 1 and Mabel's welcome. Failed writes preserve the existing campaign.
15. Reset clears progress in every area, trial records, pending play and simulated purchase/ad state while keeping Sound and Reduced Motion. Stale requests cannot restore a cleared simulated benefit.
16. First updated launch preserves the previously selected older campaign in the canonical save. Later launches use that canonical save and never re-import old progress after a reset. Settings contains no profile selector or Main/Fresh Test control.
17. The first actual time circle pauses its own lifetime and all gameplay clocks. Its spotlight tracks the transformed live target. Hover without a click, touch and touch-drag each collect through the real capped reward path; text taps and keyboard advance cannot substitute for collection.
18. Pickup collection resumes play and grants time once, including after a paused-run restore. Missing targets recover safely. Later pickups do not repeat the lesson, while Help replay and Reset progress make it eligible again.
19. At three seconds or less, each displayed decrement produces one size pop and alternating twist, with a stronger angle for each lower number and red fading softer through the second. A bonus cannot rewind or restart the beat. Reduced Motion is static. Pause freezes animation, a mid-second reload avoids a full pop, added time above three seconds restores normal presentation, and cleared-tray catch animation does not show urgency.
20. A finished campaign run compares its precise base earnings with the prior finished run in the same area. More is green, less is red, and first/equal/zero-baseline cases use truthful text without an invalid percentage. Tiny nonzero changes preserve their direction.
21. Test rewards, toolkit benefits, milestone conversions and wallet fractions affect the normal payout only; they cannot alter the base-earnings comparison. Collect still pays once and matches the large credits-to-collect total.
22. Only Cookies/Wafers cleared and Best chain remain as extra stats, with quiet previous values. The illustrated scene, dish, live payout, signed comparison and unboxed stats have a clear hierarchy at both target sizes. Every label remains readable against the art; Collect and the quieter test-bonus action remain clear, and Mabel's existing Collect target is unchanged.
23. History survives restart and area switching, keeps Bakery and Gelato separate, ignores Trials, and records clear/timeout/early-finish results once. Reopening an uncollected result retains its original previous-run comparison.
24. Missing or malformed optional history cannot invalidate otherwise valid progression. A successful Reset progress clears history, while a failed reset write preserves it with the rest of the campaign.

Automated checks demonstrate state and input behavior. A rendered preview demonstrates layout. Neither establishes comprehension, delight, retention, physical-phone touch quality or the timing targets; those require a first-time human playtest.

## Later host moments

These are follow-up design opportunities, not part of the required opening implementation: a single contextual message when a new cookie type first appears, a free Treat voucher becomes available, a second equipment slot unlocks, or the player reaches the next area's gate. Introduce each system at its first useful moment, with the same host and focused visual grammar. Keep completed guidance dismissible and avoid replaying Mabel for every ordinary run or purchase.
