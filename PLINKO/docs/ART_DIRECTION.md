# Sugarfall art direction

Revision 27 · 3 November 2026 · Art and UI production guide

**A little bakery machine made from mint enamel, warm ceramic, copper, and freshly baked food.** Objects feel rounded, useful, and touchable. Rich food colors sit against quiet sage-green surfaces in Bakery, cool lavender surfaces in Gelato Garden, and pale aqua sugar-glass surfaces in Crystal Candyworks. Gelato’s upgrade garden uses a pale ivory-and-mint ceramic courtyard; elsewhere ivory belongs to small readable panels and ceramic details; copper remains a restrained accent. The player should see the food, the next choice, and its value before noticing the interface around them.

This is the visual specification for new art. The observations below describe the inspected gameplay assets; the production and UI rules establish the direction for this UI pass and future additions. A rule in this document is an acceptance target, not a claim that every older screen already follows it.

## Core rules

1. **One material family.** Extend the ceramic, enamel, copper, parchment, wood, and food vocabulary. New metal should be copper or warm brass; new paper should feel like a recipe card.
2. **Food first.** Keep panels and paths quieter than foods, rewards, and selected choices. A UI plate must not be as visually busy as a cookie.
3. **Readable before decorative.** The silhouette and broad shading must survive reduction to final game size. Texture should enrich a form, not supply its only identity.
4. **One light direction.** Preserve upper-left highlights and lower-right shade. Do not mix photoreal product lighting, hard rim lights, or neon glow into the same screen.
5. **Natural proportions.** Preserve aspect ratio for food, icons, and ornamental objects. Stretch only a designed plain center, using slices for scalable panels and buttons. The hopper must remain deep enough to read as a funnel, not a flattened serving tray.
6. **Live information stays live.** Generate art without text, prices, counters, state marks, or progress. Render those through Godot so they stay legible, localizable, and correct.
7. **Independent paths, one preceding upgrade.** Feed 1 opens three mixed routes in Bakery and Crystal, and two in Gelato. Every later ordinary node has one drawn parent. Show owned nodes and eligible next choices, including both children of a fork; hide deeper nodes and connections. Gelato uses exact-node history rather than a family-tier prefix to open paths. Never cross connectors or hide them beneath unrelated tiles, whether straight in Bakery, faceted in Crystal or sampled curves in Gelato. Use a subtle matching outline on the selected node's visible parent, with no ingredient list or joined-requirement copy. Validate all 62 ordinary positions and each area's special nodes using its graph/layout and live map tests after an edit.

## Shape, perspective, and finish

### Food and gameplay objects

- Donuts remain circular, with a real transparent central hole, visible icing, and only a few large sprinkles at small size.
- Pomegranate remains mostly round, with a short crown and readable red arils. Split seeds are compact red teardrops.
- Pumpkin is slightly larger than an ordinary donut in gameplay, with broad lobes and a short stem. Do not exaggerate its size in a way that misrepresents its collision shape.
- Cookie pegs retain a shared circular scalloped biscuit base. Icing/material changes identify the role: cream petals, heavy chocolate armor, pistachio/gold special, clock face.
- Cookie damage uses individual PNGs: intact, chipped after the first hit, cracked at two-thirds HP or lower, and crumbling at one-third HP or lower. Each family keeps one shared canvas frame across all four states so missing pieces never cause the remainder to grow or recenter. Make damage readable at 49 board pixels and about 31 phone pixels through missing silhouette chunks and broad transparent gaps. Do not draw peg-health rings or meters, including during the Home-to-play transition. The falling time pickup's lifetime indicator is a separate functional cue.
- Cup mouths overlap the tray's lower edge; the bodies sit below it. Keep the open rim clear so the collection point is visually obvious. Multiplier text occupies the quiet front face.
- Hopper outlet points downward at the food spawn position. It must not look like a decorative cup sitting sideways.

### UI objects

- Primary action: a low, rounded mint-enamel plaque with a restrained copper lip. Large quiet center, soft highlight near the top, short lower shadow.
- Secondary action: an ivory ceramic or parchment plaque using the same corner softness and depth. No competing decorative centerpiece.
- Information card: cream recipe-card or ceramic plate, with a thin edge and very low-contrast texture. Preserve a broad blank interior.
- Circular controls: small ceramic medallions with a readable center. Upgrade nodes use rounded rectangular enamel tiles, with a food portrait, price or ownership mark, and a separate selection rim.
- Card front faces should be almost orthographic. A small bevel can imply depth; strong perspective makes aligned text look detached.
- Use rounded corners and broad rims. Avoid sharp sci-fi panels, wireframe decoration, black cartoon strokes, bevels on every text glyph, and miniature food piles around every label.

### Backgrounds and landmarks

- Use a full-bleed portrait environment, with no inset frame or blank border. Sage-green worktops and ground establish Bakery; cool lavender stone and surfaces establish Gelato Garden; pale aqua sugar glass establishes Crystal Candyworks. Carry the area color through Home, play, journey and non-map backgrounds, including the large tray center. Use ivory for small readable panels and ceramic details, with restrained copper accents.
- Confine recognizable bowls, cloth, utensils or donut slivers to clipped outer edges. Keep the central play/control area broad, pale and low contrast. Detailed props must never substitute for a functional button or compete with moving food.
- Background source images contain no route, node, text, price, button or board. Godot draws those independently. Scenery is allowed to remain fixed beneath a scrolling map; the winding route and level medallions move together.
- Backgrounds fill the physical viewport using an aspect-preserving cover crop, including space outside the logical phone safe area. Important scenery cannot depend on a corner remaining fully visible at every aspect ratio.
- Isolated landmarks use genuine alpha and retain their natural proportions. The small bakery building above level 12 is visual context, not an unlocked next-area destination.

## Low-text layout

The design canvas is **720 × 1280** and scales with the viewport. Review at that size and at the project's default **450 × 800** window. The art should not require a full-size desktop preview to be understood.

### Shared placement rules

- Use a 12 px spacing rhythm: 12 px small gaps, 24 px panel padding, 36–48 px main page margins. Gameplay may use tighter margins to give the tray room.
- Give each screen one primary action. Secondary controls should be visibly quieter, not just a different word on an identical mint button.
- Keep menu hit targets at least 72 × 72 base pixels, approximately 45 × 45 at the default window scale. Visible icons can be smaller than their hit target.
- Protect at least 20–24 px between text and a panel's decorative inner rim. A generated blank center is functional space, not an invitation to add flourishes.
- Keep modal content inside the viewport safe area. Align cards, labels, and controls to a few shared edges; do not center every unrelated block independently.
- Put numbers next to the object they describe. Avoid repeating a value in a card, a caption, and a button unless the repetition prevents an actual mistake.
- Preserve readable state differences: current, available, owned, locked, and disabled. Color alone is insufficient; pair it with an icon, mark, border, or short word.
