Feed 1 is the central entry into a taller, narrower garden of winding routes. Each main path has 14 upgrades after the shared starter, with genuine forks after steps 3, 6 and 9. Six terminal branches contain 6/6/5 upgrades on the left and 6/6/4 on the right. Bakery retains its original three-route arc.

Upgrade types use a fixed authored shuffle. Their positions stay the same between visits, saves and restarts; opening the map consumes no random numbers. Both main paths mix upgrade families, and each terminal branch contains 4–6 distinct families. The table lists purchase types, not fixed effect tiers:

| Side | Main path, grouped after fork steps 3, 6 and 9 |
| --- | --- |
| Left | Power → Feed → Bounce / Power → Gold → Power / Time → Feed → Power / Impact → Power → Power → Ricochet → Time |
| Right | Time → Gold → Feed / Ricochet → Time → Split / Bounce → Gold → Feed / Impact → Time → Feed → Gold → Time |

At each fork the terminal branch follows the incoming tangent while the continuing route turns onto what appears to be a side branch. That branch grows into another fork. The six `spine_handoffs` in the layout record identify these real purchase connections. Curved links and winding positions soften the preceding straight antler silhouette. Both choices share the same tile, link and reveal treatment, without labels announcing which continues farther.

All 62 original family/ordinal IDs, upgrade effects, caps and price formulas remain. Each node after Feed 1 has one displayed parent. Exact node ownership now controls discovery, while aggregate family counts control effects and prices. A family's slots can appear on independent branches without imposing an invisible family-order requirement. Either main path can finish without buying the other path or optional branches. Six Power purchases are reachable by the left main path's twelfth step, so Dual Shot does not require clearing every side branch.

The left main path still offers earlier direct Power investment, but both sides and their branches now mix functions. This ordering is a starting design for phone playtesting, not a measured claim about equal strength or time to complete.

## Purchases and discovery

Owned upgrades stay visible. Only the next eligible upgrades are revealed, even if the wallet cannot afford them; deeper nodes and links stay hidden. Buying Feed 1 reveals both trunk heads without choosing one. Buying a fork reveals both choices and keeps the purchased node selected. A sole newly revealed successor is selected and framed clear of fixed controls. Every purchase needs a separate tap. A dead end, failed purchase or already-visible legacy gap does not select an unrelated upgrade. Reduced Motion frames immediately.

Buying a node grants one next aggregate tier of its family at that family's current price. Buying one Power choice never grants a different Power node on another branch. Existing saves retain their exact upgrade counts and wallets through deterministic migration to node IDs, even where the shuffled map places a missing parent before an already-owned node. Gelato Twin launchers retain their Power reset: the exact previously purchased Power nodes remain visible and rebuildable, and their historical ownership keeps discovered routes open. An eligible, affordable purchase is always green, including a Power rebuild and developer-money purchase. Unaffordable purchases are red, locked nodes neutral and owned nodes cream. See [node ownership and migration](GELATO_NODE_OWNERSHIP.md).

Dual Shot (Twin launchers) is an optional map node directly below Feed 1, visible from the initial view even while locked. Its displayed link connects to Feed 1; purchase still requires both tray 8 and Power 6, and costs 3,000 coins. Existing Twin owners keep the upgrade even below these gates and after the Power reset. It uses the same moving map, selection, state colors and separate Buy action as the other nodes; neither main path requires buying it. The opening view frames Feed 1, both route heads and Dual Shot together.

Upgrade cards describe the function in a short sentence, with the current or next aggregate family tier and actual price. Gelato tiles do not show the internal slot ordinal: a node's identity does not determine its effect tier. Cards omit percentage gains and decimal performance statistics. Twin's card explains the second launcher, slower individual firing and Power reset/rebuild tradeoff. Text fits inside inset bounds clear of the illustrated frame.

During play, both Twin launchers show their own curved dotted guide: mint for the left and berry for the right. Each uses its actual launch velocity and gravity, stopping at the first peg or shared reachable target. Single-launcher aiming keeps the existing purple guide.

## Developer testing

Open **Home menu → Settings → Developer settings** to switch areas and turn **Infinite money** on for the active area. It bypasses affordability for upgrade and Twin purchases without adding or spending real coins. Unlock requirements, caps and the Twin Power reset still apply. The shop wallet shows `∞ · Developer money` while enabled.

The toggle is independent per area and lasts for the current app session. Restarting or Reset progress turns it off. Upgrades purchased while it is on are ordinary saved upgrades. Normal release builds disable the feature, and fixed-loadout Trials never receive free purchases. See [developer settings and save behavior](DEVELOPER_AREAS.md).
